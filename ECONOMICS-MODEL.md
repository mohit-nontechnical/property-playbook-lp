## The one idea

The newsletter is not the business. It is the trust machine that feeds the business. You walk one audience down three stacked layers, and almost all the money lives in the third.

| Layer | What it is | Job |
|-------|-----------|-----|
| 1. Newsletter | Property Playbook on beehiiv | Build trust at scale. Self-funding via Boosts. |
| 2. Education ladder | $299 course / $2,500 cohort / $4,000 1:1 | Pay operating costs and qualify high-intent buyers. |
| 3. Syndication | Deals raised with investor capital | The $1M and beyond. Fees plus carry. |

---

## Layer 1 — Acquisition should cost you nothing

Meta CAC is roughly $2.50/sub. Stacked beehiiv Boosts pay roughly $5.00/sub. Net CAC is negative, so you get paid to grow the list. The list is not a cost center. It is the only free customer-acquisition machine you will ever own. Spend more here as long as Boost quality holds.

## Layer 2 — The education ladder funds the operation and builds the LP list

Confirmed pricing:

- The Playbook (course): **$299**
- Deal Teardown cohort: **$2,500**
- 1:1 Mentorship: **$4,000**
- Optional one software product: **The Honest Underwriter, ~$29/mo** (the anti-pro-forma deal analyzer; the only SaaS where the contrarian brand is an unfair advantage). Build this one or none. Skip the other tool ideas to stay focused.

This layer will never be the $1M engine on its own, and that is fine. Its real job: a $4,000 mentorship client who is accredited is not the top of the LP funnel. He is the bottom. He already paid, watched you underwrite for months, and trusts you.

## Layer 3 — Syndication is the scale engine

Fees are charged on capital raised from investors (LPs):

- Acquisition fee: 1% of capital raised
- Asset-management fee: 2% per year on AUM
- Disposition fee: 1% at sale
- Carry / promote: 20% of profit

Three formulas to reason without the tool:

1. **Steady fees per year = annual capital raised × (acquisition% + hold years × mgmt%).**
   At 1% + 5 × 2%, that is `raised × 0.11`. Every $1M raised per year matures into ~$110k/yr of recurring fees.
2. **AUM plateaus at annual raise × hold years.** Raise $4M/yr on a 5-year hold and AUM caps at $20M, so recurring fees plateau near $440k/yr. To get recurring fees alone to $1M you need to raise roughly **$9-10M/yr** (about three $3M deals).
3. **Carry per exit = equity raised × (multiple − 1) × carry%.** Even at $4M/yr and a 1.8x, each exit pays `$4M × 0.8 × 20%` = **$640k in one lump**. Carry is where the real wealth is. Fees keep the lights on. The promote builds net worth.

## The trust → LP funnel

How an audience becomes raised capital. Example on a 10,000-person list at rates your record justifies:

| Stage | Rate | Result |
|-------|------|--------|
| List | — | 10,000 |
| Accredited + passive-interested | 8% | 800 LP-suitable |
| Join a deal room / webinar | 25% | 200 prospects |
| Actually invest (your close) | 15% | **30 LPs** |
| Average check $100k | — | **$3M first raise** |
| Reinvest ~3 deals | — | **$9M lifetime capital** |

**Each qualified subscriber is worth about $300 of first-raise capital** (`$100k × 8% × 25% × 15%`). To raise $10M/yr you need roughly **33,000 qualified subs** at these rates, reachable on a negative-net-CAC ad budget over time.

But you may never need that many. **LPs recycle.** Once you have 40 investors who have made money with you, you have a permanent $4-12M-per-deal machine that does not depend on list growth. The list refills the top. The relationships are the asset.

### Your unfair advantages (from the about-mohit record)

- **~45 units operated.** LPs can underwrite you as an operator, not a guru. This is why a 15% close is realistic where a no-name would be at 2-3%.
- **Enterprise AE at Salesforce.** Raising capital is closing. Most syndicators are operators who cannot sell. You are a closer who can operate.

## Sequencing (respecting the real constraints)

The W-2 is the floor and nothing compromises it. Near-term goal is $50k via consulting + course. Syndication is the year-2-plus play and needs securities counsel and a packaged track record before it touches an ad.

1. **Now:** newsletter consistency, land consulting + course revenue, hit $50k. Start tagging which subscribers are accredited.
2. **Next:** run the cohort and mentorship. Build relationships and a small, warm LP list.
3. **Later:** package the 45-unit track record, get securities counsel, run a first raise from the LPs you already qualified.

You are building the LP list a year before you raise from it.

## Compliance notes

- Not financial advice. Do your own due diligence. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
- Raising capital from a newsletter audience implicates SEC Reg D: 506(b) bars general solicitation (a problem if you advertise), 506(c) allows it but requires verified accredited investors. There are also broker-dealer limits on compensating anyone for bringing in capital.
- Get securities counsel before any capital-raise language goes near an ad or the newsletter.
- Take-rates, CPMs, conversion rates, and multiples here are planning assumptions, not promises.
